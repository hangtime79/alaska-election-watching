Alaska House: is Begich's lead safe?

Nobody disputes the count here. The question is simpler: does any reading of the ballots, counted or still to come, put Hill ahead of Begich, and what would it take? Every signed number is Hill minus Begich: positive is Hill ahead, negative is Begich ahead. Hill is an independent endorsed by the Democrats, so he is the Democratic side.

Best guess: Begich by 8,590 to 10,982, centre about 9,800. To win under Alaska's rules, Hill needs the remaining ballots to run about 13 points better for him than counted ballots of the same type (sensitivity). In 2024, Peltola's late early and absentee ballots combined ran worse for her than the ones counted first (step 3).

About half of Begich's counted lead, 6,635 of 13,347, comes from Hafner ballots that stopped. 7,228 Hafner ballots ranked no one still in the race, and 94.5% of those voters chose Peltola in the Senate final round. If every stopped ballot had followed its Senate vote, Hill would lead under Model A and trail by 756 under Model B.

Hill's column has its own outside votes. Taking off the Hill voters who chose Sullivan in the Senate final round leaves Begich ahead by 3,040 to 5,581. Taking off every Hill voter outside the Democratic ticket, as the main both-sides row does, leaves him ahead by 7,728 to 10,225 (walk 2).

The projection is a model, not a count. It covers the roughly 69,000 ballots not yet counted (step 3). Model B uses this same race in 2024, with Begich as the Republican, comparing ballots counted after 10 Nov 2024 with those counted by then. Under Model B, late absentees run about 10 points more Republican than counted ones, so every extra absentee helps Begich. That is why the absentee rows sit lower than Alaska's rules in the Model B column. On the ballots counted so far, Begich leads by 13,347.

Step 1

The count as it stands, and where the minor candidates' votes go

This step covers the ballots counted so far. Walk 1 runs the count under Alaska's rules, with no judgment calls. The tables after it follow every Hafner and McDermott ballot to where it ends up and show how the same voters marked the Senate, governor and legislative races.

Walk 1: the count as it stands

Alaska counts each ballot by its rankings. When a candidate drops out, each ballot moves to that voter's next choice, or stops if the voter ranked no one else still in the race. A ballot that reaches the final round for Hill or Begich is a two-way vote. A two-way margin is (Hill − Begich) ÷ two-way votes.

The stopped ballots, and the other side of them

Walk 2: if every stopped ballot followed its Senate vote

This walk does not change the count. Alaska's rules count stopped ballots for no one. It asks a narrower question: how much of Begich's lead comes from ballots that stopped, and how many of Hill's voters sit outside the Democratic ticket? Each stopped House ballot takes the side of the same ballot's Senate final-round vote. Then, for the second half, Hill voters outside the Democratic ticket come off. Normal stopping. Ballots always stop. In 2024, stopped House ballots that followed their presidential vote would have moved the House two-way margin by −1.74 points. In 2026 they move it by +3.87 (two-way margin −7.97 as counted against −4.10 continued). The difference comes from who stopped. In 2024 the stopped ballots leaned Republican (9,199 followed Trump, 3,094 Harris). In 2026 they lean Democratic (8,361 Peltola, 1,266 Sullivan), mostly Hafner's.

Step 3

The projection: what the uncounted ballots could add

This step is a model, not a count. It starts from the count as it stands and adds each type of uncounted ballot two ways. Model A: each type votes like counted ballots of the same type. Model B: Model A, adjusted by how ballots counted after 10 Nov 2024 differed from those counted by then, in the same race with Begich as the Republican. The best guess is the range between them under Alaska's rules.

Assumptions, and how each was tested

  • Uncounted early ballots vote like the 48,292 counted early ballots, and uncounted absentees like the 8,350 counted absentees (Model A). Tested on 2024 below: on early and absentee ballots, the same method from the 10 Nov 2024 count erred 1,604 votes toward Peltola. Model B corrects in that direction. Its central version (−2,392) uses same-district gaps; the raw gaps, the backtest's own measure, give −1,333.
  • 40% of the 18,919 unreturned absentees arrive. An assumed value, not an estimate. 0% and 100% are shown in every table.
  • About 8,000 questioned ballots, at 2024's 3.0% of all ballots, voting like all counted ballots. In 2024, questioned ballots ran 6.39 points more Republican than everything counted by 10 Nov. That is a Begich-positive scenario, worth −484.
  • The 130 overseas ballots stay out of the absentee rate. They run Hill +0.53 per ballot against +0.08 for absentees, so leaving them out is cautious for Hill.
  • The rural batch reads the same with rural or statewide election-day rates (+2,067 against +2,084, step 1).
  • Model B assumes 2026's late ballots differ from its early-counted ones as 2024's did. The race and the Republican are the same; the Democratic candidate is not. Nothing on this page tests the pattern for 2026 until more ballots are counted.
